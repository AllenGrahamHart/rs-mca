# Proof In The Original Incidence Frame

Choose a basis f_1,...,f_s of V. The credited incidence proof assigns
normal ell_x=(v(x),-f_1(x),...,-f_s(x)) and right side h_*(x)-u(x)
to coordinate x. A record is the parameter point (gamma,lambda), where
h_gamma=h_*+sum lambda_i*f_i. Independent (s+1)-tuples determine at
most one parameter point, and hence at most one original finite slope.

## 1. Finite Compatible Chart

Write c=h_*+sum mu_i*f_i. On every point of U, the parameter point
(gamma_0,mu) solves the affine incidence equation, by (COMP).

An ordered tuple entirely inside U is either dependent, so owned by no
record in the independent-tuple resource, or independent, in which case
its unique parameter solution is (gamma_0,mu). After removing gamma_0,
no retained record owns any such tuple. This removes ALL internal tuples
from the available universe, not merely the independent internal ones.

It is unnecessary to prove that gamma_0 is a bad slope or that c was its
selected explanation. If present, its whole label is charged once outside
the retained resource. The shared-domain statement alone determines the
unique solution of any internal independent tuple.

## 2. Infinite Compatible Chart

Write c=sum mu_i*f_i. On U the first normal column v is a fixed linear
combination of the remaining columns. Thus every internal (s+1)-tuple is
dependent, and no selected record can own one. No finite label is removed.
This is a rank statement in the unchanged original incidence frame.

## 3. Subtract Once And Keep All Record Weights

There are exactly (n)_falling_(s+1) ordered distinct-coordinate tuples
on the whole original domain and (|U|)_falling_(s+1) entirely inside U.
Tuples outside U's internal set may still be dependent, so retaining all
of them is a valid upper bound. Distinct selected slopes own disjoint
independent tuples by the credited theorem. This proves (REFUND), and
summing the recordwise beta*w_gamma lower bounds proves (WEIGHT).

The same beta and source must be used before and after the refund. In
particular, deleting one kernel label does not allow a different carrier,
normalization, raw-minimization rule or separately maximized resource.

The argument has no low-defect hypothesis: compatibility on U excludes
internal tuples for every retained record, even when its selected support
or minimizing pair does not belong to the low family used to define U.
This is what permits a downstream truncated-mass bound to keep all higher
defects while coupling its available resource to the actual core union.
