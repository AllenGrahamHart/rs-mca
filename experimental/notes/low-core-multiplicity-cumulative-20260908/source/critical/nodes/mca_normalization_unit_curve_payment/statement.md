# Polynomial families controlled by a normalization unit

Status: PROVED, 2026-09-07.

Let Q in F(X)[U,V] define a geometrically integral affine plane curve
of total degree e. Put k=algebraic closure of F. Suppose that after
one fixed finite extension L/k(X), the normalization of this affine
curve has a regular invertible function z which is NONCONSTANT over L.
Every reduced coefficient component of bounded polynomial pairs
(a,b) in k[X]^2 satisfying Q(a,b)=0 then has dimension <=1.

In particular, the hypothesis holds if the geometric projective
normalization is P^1 and at least TWO distinct geometric points of it
lie above the line at infinity. The points are counted on the
normalization, not merely on the original singular plane curve.

For s>=1 and pairs in an affine 2s-dimensional coefficient space with original
component degrees <K, joint agreement >=m-t>=K on n distinct points
therefore gives

    M_t <= e^(2s-1)*(n-K+1)/(m-t-K+1).

The original LOW group gain obeys the existing complete-core bound
sum_(t=1)^T (n-m+t)*M_t/(t*(t+1)). For e=3, s=11,
(n,K,m)=(1048576+J,J,67472+J) and T=500 this is at most
159185671413625180. The finite consumer owns the single resource,
near allowance and mixed-factor composition.

The dimension proof works in every characteristic at this printed
unit hypothesis. It uses no quantitative divisor-pattern count,
function-field height estimate, or transformed-coordinate LIST bound.
One-place-at-infinity rational curves are not covered merely by their
rationality. Nor is an integral function sufficient without its inverse.
