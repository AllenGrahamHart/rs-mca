# Nonconstant Pencils Control Actual Regular Children

Let V be a polynomial carrier of dimension s over F and W subset V x V.
A nonconstant pencil is an F-subspace S=T*(P,Q), with coprime P,Q,
P/Q nonconstant and T a nonzero finite-dimensional polynomial space.

1. If dim T=s-1, then V=P*T+Q*T, gcd(V)=gcd(T), and s-1 is the
   largest possible nonconstant-pencil dimension. At every regular
   anchor x, S_x has dimension s-2. It remains a nonconstant pencil
   after division by the anchor or any full common carrier factor.
2. If dim V=4, dim W=5 and W contains a nonconstant pencil PLANE S,
   every regular child W_x has function-field rank TWO. A whole
   rank-one child is impossible.
3. In this5/4 case the projective carrier image lies on an F-defined
   quadric. If dim(P*T+Q*T)=4, the quadric is smooth and every inner
   projection from a curve point is birational. If that dimension is3,
   the quadric is a cone: every inner projection except possibly at its
   vertex is birational. At any actual vertex anchor the child contains
   the full nonconstant plane S.

Regular means the original regular-anchor supplier's joint-rank-two,
dimension-preserving, full-generic-projection section. No arbitrary
joint-good coordinate may replace it. Full gcds and singular curve
points are retained; the quadric itself need only be smooth at the
chosen nonvertex point. No characteristic-zero restriction is needed.

Consequently the maximal nonconstant-pencil property propagates along
a regular rank19 dimension sequence r=2s-3 until s=r=3, where every
terminal has function-field rank two and a nonconstant pencil plane.
None of these pencil subspaces is presumed occupied by actual pairs.
