# Exact Algebraic Scope Controls

All examples are over F97, using polynomial identities rather than
identifying polynomials with their value functions.

## Smooth Quadric, Singular Curve Point

Use V=span(1,X^2,X^3,X^5), the product of span(1,X^2) and span(1,X^3).
The plane generated by(1,X^2),(X^3,X^5) is nonconstant. Add
(0,1),(X^2,0),(X^5,0) to obtain a5-dimensional regular W.
At0 the child carrier is span(X^2,X^3,X^5); full division by X^2
gives span(1,X,X^3). Thus d5,m2,mu1,eta_child3. The curve point is
singular, but the quadric point is smooth and the inner map is birational.

Composing by X->X^e retains normalization e at this anchor. The scripts
check e1,2,3 exactly, with all degrees below97.

## Cone Vertex With A Genuine Degree Jump

Use V=span(1,X^3,X^5,X^7) and W spanned by

    (X^7,X^5), (X^5,X^3), (0,X^7), (1,0), (0,1).

The first two span S=X^3*span(X^2,1)*(X^2,1). Its component carrier has
dimension3 and the quadric cone has vertex at the actual coordinate0.
The whole W is regular, as is its joint child at0.

At0, V_x=span(X^3,X^5,X^7), so the full fixed divisor has degree3.
The parent has normalization1 and image degree7; after dividing X^3
the child carrier is span(1,X^2,X^4), with normalization2 and image2.
Thus mu2 is real, not a generic-degree substitution. The child retains
the entire nonconstant plane and has function-field rank two.

## Why Nonconstant And Plane Are Essential

Let V=span(1,X,X^2,X^3) and W=(V,0)+span((0,1)).
Its generic projection is full, but its regular child at0 is
(span(X,X^2,X^3),0), a WHOLE constant-direction3/3 enclosure.
W contains large constant pencils and even a single nonconstant pair
direction(X,1); neither is a substitute for a nonconstant pencil plane.

The multilevel controls also contract maximal nonconstant pencils for
every shared starting dimension4..11 down to3, using actual F97
regular coordinates. These are enclosure controls, not official
large-agreement MCA sources or Prize counterexamples.
