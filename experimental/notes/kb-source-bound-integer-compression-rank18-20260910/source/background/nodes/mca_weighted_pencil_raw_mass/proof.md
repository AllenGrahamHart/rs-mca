# Keep The Original Owners At Every Weighted Incidence

For distinct labels assigned to the SAME pair, their selected defects are
disjoint outside its complete joint core: there v-b is nonzero and
(u-a)+gamma*(v-b)=0 determines at most one finite gamma. Thus the total
raw weight of one pair is at most n-|H_f|<=n-A. This also handles an
arbitrary zero-dimensional section without a fictitious pencil direction.

## 1. One Pencil

Write its primitive relation A0*a+A1*b=Q, h=max(deg A_i), with A0,A1
coprime. The required rational-pencil theorem parameterizes its pairs by
(a,b)=(a_*,b_*)+(A1,-A0)H, with H polynomial of degree<K-h.
The complete union C satisfies the same received relation. Coprimality
means at least one A_i is nonzero at each coordinate, so there is a scalar
receiver on C for H, with at least A agreements for every represented pair.
This map is injective and preserves affine dimension v. Scalar affine
incidence gives at most((|C|-K+h+1)/(A-K+h+1))^v pairs.

At x in C, a scalar agreement for an assigned pair forces joint agreement
unless gamma=A1(x)/A0(x), with A0(x)!=0. For a nonpreferred label its
defects are therefore outside C. For each pair, all such defects total at
most e by disjointness. Multiplying the scalar pair cap by e bounds their
weight. In a constant direction there is at most one preferred finite
label for the WHOLE pencil, whose original weight is at most t. In a
nonconstant direction the preferred image has at most |C| values, with
total weight at most t*|C|. Labels are globally unique in the fixed
assignment; this preferred charge is not repeated for every pair.
Adding these charges proves both asserted pencil bounds.

If a common good anchors have already been imposed, every pair difference
vanishes there and every complete H_f contains them. Coprimality forces
their full locator to divide parameter differences H-H_*. Divide only
differences, delete those anchors from C, and define the new auxiliary
receiver by division off the anchors. Both scalar degree and agreement
drop by a, as does |C|; the displayed ratios are unchanged. There is no
claim that the receiver or a base pair is divisible by this locator.
If no positive scalar degree remains, at most one pair remains and its
weight is handled directly; a positive-dimensional section cannot occur.

## 2. Weighted Anchors

For a bad-coordinate set B of size b, double counting good joint agreements
gives

    (A-b)*sum_f omega_t(f)
       <=sum_(x outside B) sum_(f:x in H_f) omega_t(f).

If each good child has weight at most L, the right side is at most
(n-b)L. The ratio(n-b)/(A-b) increases with b<A. The required shared
carrier theorem bounds b<=K+s-r at s<r<=2s, including common zeros,
and good children have pair/shared dimensions r-2,s-1. This proves its
weighted extension; actual subsets need not span their enclosures.

For the determinant version, divide the a anchor factors from direction
pairs. Choose two with a nonzero polynomial determinant. Its degree is
at most2(K-a-1). Off the anchors, every bad joint evaluation is a root.
Together with the a prior coordinates, there are at most2K-a-2 bad
coordinates. Apply the same weighted incidence inequality. A good affine
section has dimension r-2, and its differences acquire the new anchor.
The original degree K is retained when proceeding to the next step.

No original owner weight is divided, reset or reassigned. Sequential
incidence inequalities need neither a unique formal history nor an
unproved target-specific denominator for histories.
