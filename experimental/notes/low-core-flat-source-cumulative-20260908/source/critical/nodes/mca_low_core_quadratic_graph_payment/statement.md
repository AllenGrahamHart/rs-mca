# Polynomial-graph and moving-parabola low-pair payments

## Moving quadratic direction

The [tangent equality theorem](moving_parabolas.md) handles a
nonsingular conic over F(X) with rank-one quadratic part and
quadratic direction nonconstant in X, in characteristic not two.
For a common carrier of dimension s>=2 its coefficient dimension
is <=r=floor(s/2). Degree-two sections in the original 2s pair
coordinates retain degree 2^(2s-r), and the shared JOINT algebraic
LIST lemma bounds pairs by floor(2^(2s-r)*Q^r).

For odd s, equality in the old ceil bound would force linear
parameter variation W with both quadratic coefficients times W^2
inside V. The minimal product dimension then forces their ratio
constant, a contradiction. This proof uses the parameter only for
dimension; its larger polynomial degree is never used in LIST.

Together with earlier graphs and the separate affine-product supplier,
the finite consumer now pays every individual nonsingular conic.
General multi-conic covers and arbitrary plane curves remain open.

The [two-tangent theorem](rational_coefficient_graphs.md) allows

the dimension, degree and original-label bounds below. Normalize the
variation gcd and derivative content, then use two coprime derivative

## Earlier polynomial-coefficient extension

The [polynomial-graph theorem](polynomial_graphs.md) replaces b=a^2
by b=Psi(X,a), with Psi in F[X,Y] of Y-degree e>=2 nonzero in F.
There is no X-degree bound in this exact PAIR IDENTITY formulation.
Put r_e=floor((s+1)/2) for e=2 and r_e=max(1,floor(s/2)) for e>=3.
The represented pair count is <=floor(e^(s-r_e)*Q^r_e), with the
same Q and original-coordinate full-label conversion as below.

The proof explicitly handles fixed high coefficients and cancellation by
using the highest coefficient that varies on a geometric component.
An optional weighted-degree certificate on the COMPLETE received core
union supplies the exact pair identity by a root argument. A pointwise
fit alone does not. The finite consumer pays all degrees two through
seven with one uniform total <=167137715680311148; degree two
retains <=46570195123304300 with arbitrary polynomial X-coefficients.
No universal graph description is asserted by this earlier theorem.
