The input bounds every j-dimensional subspace's nonzero evaluation
occupancy by j*h. This is weaker than an arc with fibers <=h, but
stronger than just bounding fiber size. The missing-premise control has
distinct fibers and an overpopulated rank-two subspace.

A matched core point at which V vanishes belongs to the universally
satisfied carrier zero set. At most g such points occur on this support.
The formula subtracts g before choosing any basis; it does not assert
global basepoint-freeness or discard those coordinates from the source.

At every extension step j*h bounds the ENTIRE selected linear span,
including coordinates already selected. Choosing outside it supplies
independence. New-fiber selection alone would not suffice without the
stronger arc hypothesis; the proof uses the actual span instead.

Each completed tuple has exactly one defect relative to its OWN core.
This proves the insertion multiplicity s+1. Distinct slopes cannot own
the same independent tuple, because its affine equations pin the complete
parameter vector. The older count and the new beta may overlap within
one record, so only their maximum is summed.

The min-count corollary handles HIGH in the same tuple budget. It does
not sum independently maximized LOW and HIGH family bounds. Its beta
monotonicity is justified throughout the real interval by the strict
(s+1)T bound and positive factors.

The finite consumer proves arc/fiber parameters at original coordinates
for full rational progressions, including poles and multiplier zeros.
No coordinatewise receiver descent is used. These are sufficient
structural source conditions, not coverage of arbitrary carriers.

The two small checkers reconstruct matrix ranks with different methods
and test exact row endpoints and analytic monotonicity certificates.
They are not a formal proof of the universal combinatorial argument.
