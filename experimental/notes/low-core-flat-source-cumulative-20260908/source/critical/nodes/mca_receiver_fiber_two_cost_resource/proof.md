# Proof With Full Heavy-Core Occupancy

The required receiver-fiber theorem identifies actual receiver colors
with complete pair-core intersections in A. The colors are disjoint;
there is at most one of size >a/2. Every other color has size <=a-t.
Its proved full-locator transport charges at most a-t ORIGINAL labels
and retains full-code badness on degree K-a, length n-a, agreement m-a.
Thus the entire heavy label group has size H<=Q+a-t. No child near
event is invoked.

For a LOW heavy record its complete joint core has at least m-raw>=M
points. It contains the whole actual receiver color C, of size t<=a.
Polynomial root-space capacity gives a<=K-s+1<K<=M. Consequently an
M-point subset can be chosen to contain ALL of C. Its exact intersection
with A is C, since the complete core contains no other color of A.
Universal-core emptiness makes these evaluations nonzero. The required
fiber basis count therefore gives F_A(t) ordered bases at the actual
occupancy, not a hypothetical selected-support occupancy.

The original defect can be inserted at s+1 recoverable positions in
each such basis. This is the completed-core resource: the counted core
points need not lie in the old selected witness. All tuples still identify
the original finite label. Light records similarly have intersection
between zero and a-t. Piecewise positive log-concavity of F_A gives
the printed endpoint minimum, including its kink when present.

The independent tuple sets of all original labels are disjoint. With
H heavy and L light labels, their separately proved costs give

    U >= beta_L*L+beta_H*H.

If beta_H<=beta_L, rearrange and insert the nonnegative upper bound on H.
If beta_H>beta_L, discard the resulting nonpositive coefficient of H,
using H>=0 rather than its upper bound. This proves (TWO-COST).
The positive part is necessary; inserting an upper bound into a negative
coefficient would be invalid.

With no heavy label, all cores have occupancy<=a/2 and U>=beta_L*L.
Adding a nonnegative relaxed heavy term cannot weaken that conclusion.
Boxwise costs satisfy the same inequality whenever they are uniform
lower bounds; no optimized-profile monotonicity is assumed here.

Receiver transport and the original resource are credited predecessors.
The refinement is full heavy-core occupancy and two independent cost
coefficients, not a new near theorem, field change or free child census.
