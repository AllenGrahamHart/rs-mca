# A Conic Kernel Image Has Only Two Base Points, With Multiplicity

Parametrize C over F by its complete binary quadratic system. The vector
ell cross (ell*T) consists of binary quartics. It is not identically zero
by function-field rank2. Cancel its common homogeneous factor. This gives
a basepoint-free map from P1 to a geometrically integral image K.

K is not contained in a line. Such containment would give a linear relation
among the three cross-product polynomials. Their coefficient matrix is over
F, so any geometric linear relation implies one over F. The corresponding
F-plane H contains every generic joint kernel. Evaluation restricted to H
then has rank at most1 identically, contradicting pencil-freeness. The
converse is identical, so nondegeneracy also certifies pencil-freeness.

T is not scalar. Its geometric eigenvectors lie in a finite union of proper
linear subspaces. The irreducible nondegenerate K cannot be contained in
that union. For generic y=d(p), therefore, y and Ty are independent and
p is recovered rationally as the annihilator of span(y,Ty). This proves
birationality, not merely generic injectivity on rational points. Comparing
the degree of the cancelled binary system with the image degree gives
chi=4-degree(common factor). Nondegeneracy gives2<=chi<=4.

If chi=2, the cancelled system is a basis of binary quadratics and identifies
P1 isomorphically with K. The cross-product base divisor has degree2.
Its support is exactly where ell and ell*T are dependent, since ell never
vanishes. Distinct support points number at most2, over the algebraic closure.
The extended map C->K is an F-isomorphism, so distinct nonbase points have
distinct kernel directions, INCLUDING possible F-eigenvector directions.

At a rational base point p0, ell0*T=lambda*ell0 with lambda in F; hence
H0=ker ell0 is T-invariant. The identity ell(p)*d(p)=0 holds after cancelling
the common factor and extends to p0, giving d(p0) in P(H0). A line meets
the nonsingular conic K in at most2 geometric points counted with multiplicity.
Thus at most one nonbase p has d(p) in P(H0). These are precisely the
rank-two evaluation points where the restriction to H0 is noninjective.

At a second rational base point p1, the two distinct hyperplanes H0,H1
are invariant. Their intersection is a one-dimensional F-invariant
subspace and hence an eigenvector line. This treats actual original-field
points only; nonrational base points carry no actual evaluation coordinate.
