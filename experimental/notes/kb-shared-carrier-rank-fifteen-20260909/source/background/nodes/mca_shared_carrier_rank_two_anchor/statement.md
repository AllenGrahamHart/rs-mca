# Shared-Carrier Rank Improves The Joint Anchor

Status: PROVED. Let F be any field, D consist of n distinct field
elements, and V be an s-dimensional subspace of F[X]_<K. Let
W be an r-dimensional F-subspace of V x V, where s<r<=2s.
For x in D, let ell_x in V* be evaluation at x, and let

    B={x in D : rank_F(ev_x:W -> F^2)<2}.

Common zeros of V are included in B. Then

    dim span{ell_x:x in B} <= 2s-r,
    |B| <= K+s-r.                                    (BAD)

Let Z=f_*+W be an affine pair family, with polynomial components of
degree<K. Suppose S is a finite subset of Z, each of whose pairs has
at least A joint agreements with a fixed received pair on D, where
1<=A<=n. If A>K+s-r, and each good-coordinate agreement section
contains at most L members of S for L>=0, then

    |S| <= (n-K-s+r)/(A-K-s+r) * L.                  (ANCHOR)

Every nonempty good-coordinate section is an affine (r-2)-space whose
two component directions belong to the same carrier

    V_x={v in V:v(x)=0}, dim V_x=s-1.                 (CHILD)

No coordinate, polynomial degree, agreement threshold or source label
is changed by this incidence count. The child can have common zeros.

Also, if W is nonzero, has r=s and function-field direction rank one,
its rational direction is constant: W lies on an F-defined line in
V x V. More generally a nonconstant rational direction has r<=s-1.
This last statement does not require s<r. It is the common-carrier
pencil rigidity supplied by the required rational-line theorem.

These are pair-counting and linear-algebra statements, not an MCA
payment. An actual shared carrier, the positive denominator and any
pair-to-slope accounting remain explicit consumer obligations.
