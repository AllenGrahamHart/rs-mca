## 2. The conic cases are exhaustive

In odd characteristic a degree-two affine equation has quadratic
homogeneous part of rank one or two. For a nonsingular projective
conic the following cases exhaust it:

1. Rank two: completing the square gives a nonzero homogeneous
   level with two factor directions. The affine-product theorem pays
   original total <=46075114951019479.
2. Rank one, constant quadratic direction: a fixed GL_2(F) change
   makes one component linear in its rational parameter. The other
   is a degree-two rational-X polynomial graph, paid with original
   total <=46570195123304300. Rational shifts are absorbed into
   its coefficients, not used to change the polynomial code.
3. Rank one, moving quadratic direction: section 1 pays (AC).

The field characteristic 2130706433 is not two. Nonsingularity is
over F(X) after projective homogenization, not a numerical test at
particular domain coordinates.

For completeness, an irreducible degree-two factor over F(X) that
is geometrically singular has at most ONE affine F(X)-point. Over
an algebraic extension it is two conjugate distinct lines; a rational
point on either lies on both, hence at their unique intersection.
A double line in characteristic not two would already have a linear
factor over F(X), contrary to irreducibility. Thus such a factor
has at most one represented polynomial pair and group gain <=981604.
It may be assigned the ordinary degree-two price below. Reducible
quadratics are handled by their actual linear factors, not this argument.
